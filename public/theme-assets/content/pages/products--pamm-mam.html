What Is PAMM?

PAMM stands for Percentage Allocation Management Module. It's a way of pooling investor funds under a single money manager.

Investors allocate capital to a manager's PAMM account. The manager trades that combined pool as one account. When trades close, profits and losses are shared out among investors in proportion to their share of the pool. If an investor contributed 10% of the total capital, they receive 10% of the result. The manager typically earns a performance fee based on profits, and sometimes a management fee as well.

For investors, PAMM forex is simple. They choose a manager, allocate funds and follow results. They don't need to understand position sizing or trade execution. For managers, it means trading one account instead of many.

02 / PRODUCT

What Is MAM?

MAM stands for Multi-Account Manager. It also lets one manager trade on behalf of several investors, but the structure is different.

With MAM, each investor keeps their own separate trading account. The manager places trades from a master account, and those trades are allocated across linked investor accounts according to an allocation method. That might be based on each account's equity, a fixed lot size, a percentage or another rule.

Because each investor has their own account, MAM software allows more flexibility. Different investors can have different allocation settings, risk levels or fee arrangements, and positions appear directly in their own accounts.

03 / PRODUCT

PAMM vs MAM

01

Account Structure

PAMM pools investor funds into one managed account. MAM keeps each investor in their own account, linked to a master.

02

Allocation

PAMM distributes results proportionally based on each investor's share of the pool. MAM allocates individual trades to each account according to a chosen method.

03

Flexibility

PAMM is simpler and more uniform. MAM allows more customisation per investor, such as different allocation sizes or risk settings.

04

Visibility

In PAMM, investors see their share of results. In MAM, investors see actual positions in their own accounts.

05

Best Suited To

PAMM often suits managers serving a larger number of investors with similar expectations. MAM tends to suit managers serving fewer clients who want tailored settings or direct visibility of trades.

Many brokers offer both, so managers can choose the model that suits their clients.

How It Works

The technology sits on top of your trading platform and connects to your CRM and client portal.

A trader applies to become a money manager. Once approved by your team, they set up their offer, including strategy description, fee structure, minimum investment and any conditions. Their offer appears in a manager listing or is shared through a direct link.

An investor reviews the manager's performance, chooses to invest and allocates funds from their account. The system records the allocation and links the investor to the manager.

The manager trades. In PAMM, trades happen on the pooled account and results are distributed proportionally. In MAM, trades on the master account are copied to linked investor accounts according to the allocation method.

At each settlement period, the system calculates results, deducts the agreed fees and updates investor balances. Investors can add funds, withdraw or stop following the manager, subject to the rules set for that offer.

Throughout, both sides can see their numbers in dashboards, and your team can monitor everything from the back office.

Performance Monitoring

Trust is everything in money management. Investors need to see accurate, honest performance before they commit and while they're invested.

The system tracks each manager's results over time, including returns, drawdowns, trading history, number of investors and capital under management. Performance is calculated from actual trading data rather than self-reported figures. Investors see their own results separately from the manager's overall performance, since timing of investment affects individual outcomes.

Your team gets reporting across all managers and investors, including total allocated capital, fee income, trading volume and activity patterns.

01

Investors can set limits, such as a stop-loss level on their investment, so their allocation is withdrawn automatically if losses reach a chosen point.

02

Manager Limits

You can set rules on leverage, maximum drawdown or allowed instruments for managed accounts.

03

Monitoring and Alerts

Your team can monitor managers whose trading behaviour changes sharply or whose drawdowns grow, and act before problems spread to many investors.

04

Every allocation, trade distribution, fee and withdrawal is recorded, which helps with disputes and audits.

Fee Calculation

Fees are one of the most sensitive parts of any money management offering. The system calculates performance fees, management fees and any other agreed charges automatically, at the settlement periods defined for each offer. High-water mark logic can be applied so managers only earn performance fees on new profits, not on recovering previous losses. Fee history is visible to both managers and investors, which keeps the arrangement transparent.
