What Is Forex Liquidity?

In simple terms, liquidity is the ability to buy or sell at a quoted price. For a brokerage, it means having a reliable source of executable prices and, depending on your model, somewhere to send client orders.

That liquidity comes from a liquidity provider. These are typically banks, prime brokers, prime-of-prime firms and non-bank market makers. Each provider streams prices for the instruments it supports and accepts orders under the terms agreed with your brokerage.

Different providers offer different instruments, pricing depth, execution policies and commercial terms. Many brokers use more than one, either to widen their instrument range, improve pricing or keep a backup in place.

One thing worth saying clearly: we're a technology provider, not a liquidity provider. We don't supply prices or take the other side of trades. Our role is building, configuring and maintaining the connection between your platform and the providers you work with, and helping you understand the technical side of choosing them.

How Connectivity Works

Picture two systems that speak different languages. On one side, your MT4 or MT5 server, which manages client accounts, positions and orders. On the other, one or more liquidity providers, each with their own systems, formats and instrument naming.

Connectivity technology sits in the middle and translates.

Prices flow from your providers, through the connection layer, into your trading platform, where they're shown to clients according to your markups and settings. When a client places an order, it travels the other way: from the platform, through the connection layer, to the provider selected by your routing rules. The provider executes, confirms and sends back an execution report, which updates the client's position.

That connection layer is usually a liquidity bridge, a platform gateway, a direct FIX API connection or a combination. Which one fits depends on your platform, your providers and your business model.

Liquidity Bridge

A liquidity bridge is software that connects your trading platform to one or more liquidity providers. It's the workhorse of most brokerage connectivity setups.

The bridge receives price streams from providers and delivers them to your platform. It takes client orders from the platform and routes them to the right destination. It processes execution reports and updates positions. And it's where much of your execution logic lives, including routing rules, markups and risk settings.

For MetaTrader brokers, an MT5 liquidity bridge or MT4 bridge is often the most practical option, because it handles the translation between the platform and providers that may not connect to MetaTrader directly. A well-configured bridge also gives your dealing or risk team visibility over what's flowing through it.

We install, configure and maintain the bridge as part of your liquidity solution, and adjust it as your providers, instruments or business model change.

Aggregation

Liquidity aggregation combines price streams from several providers into one view, then routes orders based on the rules you set.

There are a few reasons brokers aggregate. Pulling prices from several sources can improve the depth and competitiveness of the quotes your clients see. It reduces dependence on a single provider, so if one connection has problems, pricing can continue from the others. And it gives you more flexibility in how orders are handled, for example routing by price, by provider, by instrument or by order size.

Aggregation does add complexity. More providers mean more symbols to map, more connections to monitor and more rules to manage. It's worth it for many brokers, but not every brokerage needs it from day one. We'll help you decide what makes sense at your stage.

FIX API

FIX, short for Financial Information eXchange, is a standard messaging protocol used widely across financial markets for prices, orders and execution reports.

Many liquidity providers offer FIX API connections, and some setups use direct FIX sessions instead of, or alongside, a bridge. FIX can also be useful for connecting other systems, such as risk tools, reporting systems or institutional clients who want direct access.

FIX API forex connections need careful setup. Session parameters, message formats and the specific way each provider implements the standard all need to be configured and tested. We handle that technical work and make sure sessions behave reliably, including how they reconnect after a drop.

MT4/MT5 Integration

Your liquidity setup has to fit properly with your MetaTrader environment, and that's where details matter.

Symbols on your platform need to line up with symbols at each provider. Trading groups need to route to the right destinations. Execution settings need to reflect your model. Margin and contract settings need to match what providers actually trade. If MT4 and MT5 run side by side, both need to connect cleanly to the same liquidity infrastructure.

MT5 offers native gateway options for some connections, while many setups rely on a bridge. MT4 almost always depends on a bridge for external liquidity. We'll recommend the approach that suits your platform and providers rather than forcing one method onto every broker.

Symbol Mapping

This is one of the least visible parts of the job and one of the most important.

Providers don't all name or structure instruments the same way. One might call it EURUSD, another EUR/USD, another EURUSD.raw. Contract sizes can differ. So can price digits, minimum volumes and trading hours. Your platform needs every one of those details matched correctly for every instrument, at every provider.

When mapping is wrong, the results are serious. Prices display incorrectly, orders go to the wrong instrument or in the wrong size, and positions don't reconcile. We map each symbol carefully, document the configuration and verify it against live provider data before launch. When you add new instruments or providers later, the same checks apply.

09 / SOLUTION

Order Routing

Routing rules decide what happens to each client order. They're how your execution model is put into practice.

In an A-book model, orders are passed to liquidity providers. In a B-book model, the broker manages the risk internally. Many brokers run a hybrid, routing based on factors like client group, instrument, order size or trading behaviour.

We configure routing to reflect the model you've chosen and the risk approach your team has decided on. Rules can be adjusted as your business changes, and we make sure any change is tested before it affects live orders. The decisions about your execution model and risk policy are yours. Our role is making sure the technology carries them out correctly.

Price Validation and Execution Testing

Before going live, we put the connection through its paces.

Price feeds are checked against provider data for accuracy and consistency. Test orders confirm that routing, execution and reporting work as expected. We also test the awkward cases: rejections, partial fills, requotes where relevant, disconnections and what happens when a provider stops streaming. Knowing how the system behaves under pressure is as important as knowing it works on a calm day.

11 / SOLUTION

Monitoring

Once live, liquidity connections need constant attention. We monitor price feeds for stale or frozen quotes, connection status for drops and reconnections, execution behaviour for unusual rejection rates or delays, and bridge health overall.

Alerts are set up so problems are spotted quickly, often before clients notice. Where backup routing is in place, monitoring helps confirm that failover works as planned. Over time, monitoring data also helps you understand how each provider is performing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are you a liquidity provider?

No. We're a technology provider. We build and manage the connection between your platform and your chosen liquidity providers.

Can you help me choose a liquidity provider?

We can explain the technical factors worth comparing, such as connection methods, instrument coverage and platform compatibility. The commercial relationship and final choice are yours.

What's the difference between a liquidity bridge and FIX API?

A liquidity bridge is software that connects your platform to providers and manages routing and execution logic. FIX API is a messaging protocol that can be used for direct connections. Many setups use both.

Do I need liquidity aggregation?

Not always. It's useful for improving pricing depth and adding redundancy, but it adds complexity. Many brokers start with one or two providers and add aggregation later.

Can I connect more than one provider?

Yes. Multiple providers with aggregation or backup routing is a common setup.

Does this work with both MT4 and MT5?

Yes. We connect both platforms to liquidity, including setups where both run side by side.

Can you set up A-book, B-book or hybrid routing?

Yes. We configure routing to match your chosen execution model and risk approach.

Do you guarantee spreads or execution quality?

No. Pricing and execution depend on your providers and market conditions. We make sure the technology connecting you to them is configured and maintained properly.

Can I add new instruments or providers later?

Yes. New providers and instruments go through the same mapping and testing process before going live.
